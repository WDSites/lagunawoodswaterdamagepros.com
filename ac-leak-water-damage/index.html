AC leak water damage is moisture damage caused by an air conditioner or heat pump releasing water where it should not go. Cooling equipment pulls water out of the air as it runs, and that condensate has to leave through a drain line. In Laguna Woods, CA, when that line clogs or a pan overflows, the water goes into the ceiling, wall or floor around the equipment.

Call (949) 670-0939 for the water damage. We extract the water, dry the materials and repair drywall and paint. We do not repair air conditioners, so an HVAC technician has to fix the unit, and the leak keeps returning until they do.

Where AC water comes from

Clogged condensate drain line

Algae and debris build up in the drain pipe, and the water backs up into the pan and then over its edge.

Overflowing drain pan

A rusted or cracked pan, or a full one, spills onto the ceiling below an attic air handler or onto a closet floor.

Frozen evaporator coil

Low airflow or a dirty filter can ice up the coil. When it melts, more water arrives than the pan can handle.

Ductless mini-split drip

A wall unit with a blocked drain or a tilted body drips down the wall and stains the paint and baseboard.

Air handler in a closet or attic

These sit above living space or against walls, so a small leak reaches drywall quickly.

Why AC leaks show up in the warm season

Laguna Woods has a dry coastal-inland climate, and the air conditioner runs mostly in the long warm season. NOAA's 1991 to 2020 climate normals for the nearest long-record station, Santa Ana John Wayne Airport, show 11.18 inches of precipitation a year. That station is not in Laguna Woods itself, so use it as a regional guide only.

Rain is rare in summer, with June, July and August each averaging well under a tenth of an inch at that station. A ceiling stain that appears then points to plumbing or air conditioning, not weather. Look at the thermostat and the air handler first, then call a plumber if the unit is dry.

Safety steps when an AC unit is leaking

  • Turn the AC off at the thermostat, then switch off power to the unit at the breaker before you touch wet equipment.
  • Never stand in water near the air handler, outlets or the electrical disconnect. Call 911 if you feel a shock risk, see sparks or smell burning.
  • Place a bucket under the drip and move furniture and electronics away from the wet zone.
  • Photograph the unit, the drain line, the pan and the stain, with the date visible.
  • In Laguna Woods Village, report the leak to Resident Services at 949-597-4600 and ask which party handles the equipment.
  • Call an HVAC technician to stop the source. Do not run the system again until it is checked.

What AC water does to your home

Condensate is usually clean at first, but standing water in a drain pan can grow slime and smell. It soaks into the paper face of drywall and into insulation, then spreads along the surface of the ceiling. Left in place, damp drywall can support mold growth, because the EPA says mold needs moisture to grow.

A slow drip is easy to miss. A closet air handler may leak for weeks behind a door or inside a wall. Check the floor around the unit and the wall behind it for soft spots, bubbling paint or a musty smell.

Our drying and repair process

Confirm the source is off

The AC stays off until an HVAC technician clears it, so we are not drying with a leak still running.

A moisture meter and a thermal camera map the wet drywall, insulation and flooring, including areas that look dry.

What affects the cost of AC leak water damage repair

  • How long the leak ran. A slow drip over weeks soaks deeper than a one-day overflow.
  • Where the unit sits. Attic and closet units put water into ceilings, walls and floors, and each needs its own steps.
  • Materials affected. Drywall, insulation, carpet and cabinetry are handled differently.
  • Size of the wet area. More square footage means more drying equipment and more time.
  • Finish matching. Texture and paint that must blend with the room add repair steps.

Insurance is worth a short, general note. California's Department of Insurance says homeowners policies do not cover flood damage but do cover other kinds of water damage, and coverage depends on the cause. Sudden and accidental water damage is often covered, while slow leaks and neglect often are not, and flood usually needs its own policy. Check your policy or ask your insurer, because we cannot promise how any claim will be decided.

A sudden overflow is often treated differently from a leak that dripped for months. Many policies exclude damage from long-term seepage or poor maintenance, so check your policy and ask your insurer. Keep the HVAC technician's invoice, since it can show when and why the leak started.

Prevent the next leak

Schedule HVAC service

A technician can clear the condensate line, check the pan and test the float switch before the warm season.

Change filters

A dirty filter restricts airflow and can ice the coil, which melts into the pan.

Add a pan alarm

A water sensor in the drain pan warns you before it reaches the ceiling.

Check the drain end

Look for dripping at the outdoor termination. Water there means the line is moving.
