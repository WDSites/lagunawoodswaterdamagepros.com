If water is on the floor right now, treat it as an emergency. Our dispatch line, (949) 670-0939, is open 24/7, so you can call at 2 a.m. on a Sunday and reach a person. While you wait for a crew, shut off the water at the fixture's stop valve or the main valve, lift small items off the floor and keep people and pets out of the water.

Emergency water removal, also called water extraction, is the removal of standing and absorbed water with pumps, extraction wands and portable extraction units. It is the first physical step in restoration and it comes before drying and repair. The EPA advises drying water-damaged areas and items within 24 to 48 hours to prevent mold growth, which is why the clock starts the moment the water stops.

When standing water is an emergency

Some water problems can wait until morning. Others cannot. The situations below are the ones that need a call right away, whatever the hour.

The leak is still running

A failed supply line, water heater or toilet connection keeps adding water until it is shut off. Stop the source first if you can reach the valve safely, then call.

Water has reached outlets or appliances

Water near electrical outlets, the panel or plugged-in appliances is a shock risk. Stay out of the room and see the safety box below before you do anything else.

The water looks dirty or smells

Gray or black water from a backed-up drain, a washing machine overflow or sewage is contaminated. Do not wade in it and do not try to clean it up yourself.

Water is coming through the ceiling

In a stacked building the source may be in the unit above. Document it with photos, move belongings and call. The ceiling can hold a surprising amount of water before it gives way.

Safety first, before you call the restoration line

  • If water touches an outlet, a power strip or the electrical panel, stay out of the room. Cut power at the panel only if you can reach it without stepping in water.
  • For any sign of an electrical or gas problem, call 911 or the utility before you call us. Southern California Edison is the electric provider named on the City of Laguna Woods utility page.
  • Keep children, older family members and pets away from sewage or dirty water. Leave the cleanup of contaminated water to a crew with protective gear.
  • Do not use a household vacuum on water. A wet-dry vacuum rated for liquids is the only kind that is safe for small spills.

How emergency water removal works

1

Stop and size up

The crew confirms the source is off and checks how far the water has traveled, including under walls and into the next room.

2

Extract

Wands and pumps pull bulk water from carpet, padding and hard floors. Deep puddles go first.

3

Map the moisture

Moisture meters and thermal imaging show wet areas that look dry, such as the base of a wall or the layer under flooring.

4

Hand off to drying

Once standing water is out, air movers and dehumidifiers take over. See water mitigation for that stage.

Extraction removes far more water than towels or a mop can. A single flooded room can hold hundreds of gallons across the carpet, pad and floor edges, and most of it sits where you cannot see it. Padding under carpet acts like a sponge and dries slowly, so a crew may lift it, while the carpet itself can often be saved if it is lifted and treated quickly.

Hard floors need a different touch. Water can slip between planks or tiles and sit on the subfloor, so a crew checks beneath the surface before calling a room dry.

Burst pipe water damage

A burst pipe is the classic extraction call. Pressurized supply lines can move a lot of water in minutes, so the first move is the shutoff valve, either at the fixture, at the unit's valve or at the main. After that, call us and photograph the damage before anything is moved.

In a manor or condominium, the pipe may sit inside a shared wall or serve more than one unit. The Village's Resident Services line, 949-597-4600, is the number the community lists for leaks, and reporting the break there is a sensible step alongside calling us. Your mutual's own rules decide who handles the building's side of the repair, so ask.

The crew extracts the standing water, opens the wall or ceiling only where moisture readings call for it and sets up drying. Pipe repair itself is a plumber's work. Restoration deals with the water the pipe left behind.

What affects the cost of water removal

Cost depends on the job, not a flat rate. We do not quote exact prices on a web page, because the same call can be a small spill or a flooded floor. These are the factors that move the number.

  • Water category. Clean, gray and black water call for different gear, materials and disinfection.
  • Area affected. Square footage and how many rooms or floors the water reached.
  • Materials involved. Carpet, padding, hardwood, laminate, drywall and cabinetry react differently.
  • Time since the loss. Water that has sat longer soaks deeper and takes more drying days.
  • Rebuild scope. Some jobs end after drying, others need walls, floors or ceilings repaired.

Insurance is worth a short, general note. California's Department of Insurance says homeowners policies do not cover flood damage but do cover other kinds of water damage, and coverage depends on the cause. Sudden and accidental water damage is often covered, while slow leaks and neglect often are not, and flood usually needs its own policy. Check your policy or ask your insurer, because we cannot promise how any claim will be decided.

After the water is out

Extraction is the start, not the finish. Once the standing water is gone, water mitigation dries the structure, water damage cleanup removes debris and sanitizes, and water damage repair rebuilds what could not be saved. If the water came from outside during a storm, flood damage restoration covers that kind of loss.
