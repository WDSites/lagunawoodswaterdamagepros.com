Where Third Mutual sits in the Village

Third Mutual is a membership inside Laguna Woods Village, not a separate street grid with its own boundary. The Village says it was built in phases between 1963 and 1985 on 2,095 acres, with 12,736 units in 2,584 residential buildings. Its neighborhoods page says Third Mutual has many units next to the Village's 27-hole golf course.

We do not claim exact boundaries, because none were published. If you are unsure which mutual your building belongs to, check your purchase documents or ask Resident Services at 949-597-4600.

Who handles the leak, the Mutual or you

Third Mutual publishes an insurance disclosure and a damage restoration policy. As we read the disclosure, the Mutual's master policy covers units as originally built, with a per-occurrence deductible of 100,000 dollars. Interior damage from a plumbing failure in your unit or a neighbor's is generally the owner's responsibility, which means fixtures, finishes, appliances and personal property.

The damage restoration policy names causes such as water heater and toilet leaks, but our summary did not make clear which column each cause falls in. Do not rely on our reading. Call Resident Services and ask how your specific leak is classified before repairs begin.

First-hour safety steps in a condominium

  • Stop using the fixture, then close its shut-off valve if you can reach it safely. If you cannot find a valve, ask Resident Services where the building shut-off is.
  • Keep people and pets away from standing water near outlets, appliances and power strips.
  • Do not touch breakers or switches while standing in water. For a sparking outlet or a shock risk, call 911.
  • Stay out from under a ceiling that bulges or drips, because wet drywall can drop without warning.
  • Photograph the damage with the date visible, including any water that reached a neighbor's unit.
  • Report the leak to Resident Services at 949-597-4600 so the Mutual can check shared plumbing and notify neighbors.
  • If the water came up from a drain, treat it as contaminated. Do not use fans or a household vacuum on it.

Our process for a condominium leak

1

Find the wet area

Moisture meters and a thermal camera check the wall, ceiling and floor, including areas that look dry and may reach a neighbor.

2

Remove standing water

Extraction wands and pumps take water off floors, then wet carpet pad and loose materials are pulled up.

3

Open what cannot dry

Soaked drywall and insulation are cut out, which exposes the cavity so air can move through it.

4

Dry and measure

Air movers and dehumidifiers run until readings reach dry reference points, with the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard as a reference.

5

Repair with approval

Drywall, paint and flooring are repaired once you have confirmed the plan with the Mutual, because the City says HOA approvals are in addition to city requirements.

Insurance documentation for a Third Mutual loss

The California Department of Insurance advises reporting a loss promptly, keeping a log of calls and receipts for temporary repairs, and photographing the damage. It says homeowners policies do not cover flood but do cover other kinds of water damage, depending on cause and policy terms.

In a condominium, two policies can be involved: the Mutual's master policy and your own HO-6 policy, which the Mutual strongly recommends. Ask both which one applies. Photos, moisture readings and an itemized scope can describe the work, but they do not guarantee that a claim is paid.
