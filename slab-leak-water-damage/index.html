Slab leak water damage is the damage caused when a pressurized water line under or inside a concrete slab leaks into the flooring, walls and soil around it. In Laguna Woods, CA, it matters for ground-floor homes built directly on a slab, because the pipes are not in a crawl space or attic where you can see them.

If you suspect one, call a licensed plumber for the pipe and call our 24/7 line, (949) 670-0939, for the water.

How common is it here, and who is at risk?

Many Southern California homes sit on concrete slabs, and slab leaks are a known problem for slab-on-grade houses and ground-floor units in the region. We will not quote a statistic, because we do not have a verified one for the Village.

Foundation type and plumbing material are not verified for specific Village buildings. Two-story manors have no slab under the upper unit, so a slab leak cannot start there, although a ground-floor neighbor could still be affected. Ask Resident Services or your mutual what your building sits on and where its supply lines run.

Safety steps in the first hour

  • Shut off the main water valve for your home. If you cannot find it or it will not turn, call Resident Services at 949-597-4600 if you live in the Village.
  • If water reaches an outlet, appliance or the electrical panel, stay out of that room and call 911 or the utility.
  • Do not cut into the floor or break the slab yourself. Leave the pipe to a licensed plumber.
  • Move rugs, boxes and furniture off damp flooring and photograph the area before anything changes.

Who does what: the plumber and us

Leak detection and pipe repair or rerouting are done by a licensed plumber. That might mean electronic listening, pressure testing, spot repair or rerouting the line through a wall or ceiling. We do not locate or fix the pipe.

What we handle is the water damage side: extraction, drying, and removal or replacement of wet flooring, baseboards and drywall. The plumber and our crew often work in sequence, so tell each one what the other found.

How we handle the water damage

1

Extract standing water

Extraction wands pull water from carpet, padding and hard floors once the plumber has stopped the line.

2

Map the moisture

Moisture meters and a thermal camera find wet baseboards, drywall bottoms and floor layers.

3

Remove what cannot dry

Soaked padding, baseboard and the lower section of wet drywall may be removed so the rest can dry.

4

Dry the structure

Air movers and dehumidifiers run, and readings are checked until each material returns to a normal range.

5

Restore

Flooring, drywall, paint and trim are replaced after the structure is dry.

Why concrete slabs dry slowly

Concrete holds moisture and releases it slowly, so a damp slab can keep feeding the floor above it for days. Wood flooring, laminate and carpet laid over a wet slab can cup, buckle or hold dampness against the underside. A crew measures the slab itself and the finished flooring separately.

Our water mitigation work covers that drying stage, and water damage cleanup handles debris and sanitizing when the water was not clean.

What changes the cost

  • Where the water traveled. A damp corner is a different job from water that spread under an entire room.
  • Flooring type. Carpet, laminate, hardwood and tile react differently and have different repair paths.
  • Drywall and baseboard removal. Wet wall bottoms add labor and a rebuild step.
  • Drying days. Concrete and thick materials hold moisture longer and need more monitoring.
  • Plumber work. Pipe detection and repair are billed separately by the plumber, not by us.

Insurance and slab leaks

Coverage often depends on whether the leak was sudden and accidental or slow and ongoing, and on whether the policy covers the pipe, the water damage or both. Check your policy and ask your insurer, since mutuals and policies differ. We cannot promise how any claim will be decided.

The Third Mutual damage restoration policy lists slab or wall leaks among causes it addresses. Confirm who is responsible with Resident Services at 949-597-4600 and your mutual before you spend money on repairs.

Mistakes to avoid

Ignoring a rising water bill is the most common one. Pouring over a damp floor with new carpet or laminate before the slab is dry is another, and it traps moisture. Breaking into the floor yourself can damage pipes, wiring or the slab.
