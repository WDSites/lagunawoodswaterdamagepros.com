A water loss in a commercial space is a business problem before it is a building problem. If water is in your Laguna Woods, CA office, shop or suite, call our 24/7 line, (949) 670-0939. A crew can be dispatched the same day when available.

Commercial water damage restoration is the removal of water, drying of the structure, cleanup and repair after a leak or flood in a business space. We handle small commercial spaces such as offices, shops and medical or retail suites. The work follows the same steps as a home, but access, power and paperwork matter more.

Where business water losses start

Laguna Woods is a small city of about three square miles, according to the City of Laguna Woods website, and its commercial strips run along El Toro Road and Moulton Parkway. Those strips hold small buildings with shared walls, shared roofs and shared plumbing. A failure in one suite is rarely contained to that suite.

Common causes include a supply line or water heater failure, a restroom overflow, a drain backup, a roof leak after rain and a fire sprinkler or HVAC condensate line that lets go. Many happen at night, when nobody is there to notice.

What you see and what it means

Stained or sagging ceiling tiles

Tiles absorb water and can drop without warning. Keep people out from under them and call before anyone lifts them.

Rippling or squishing carpet

Commercial carpet is often glued down or laid over a thin pad. Water gets underneath and spreads well past the wet spot.

Swollen baseboards or wall bottoms

Drywall wicks water upward. The visible line is usually lower than the real wet zone.

Water in the neighboring suite

Water has crossed a shared wall or ceiling. Both suites need moisture readings, and the property manager needs a call.

Commercial water loss safety steps

  • If it is safe, shut off the water at the fixture's stop valve or the building main. If you cannot find it, call the property manager or landlord and ask who holds the shutoff locations.
  • Do not enter a room with water near outlets, panels or powered equipment. Southern California Edison is the electric provider and SoCalGas is the gas provider, according to the City of Laguna Woods utility page, so call them for any power or gas concern.
  • Call 911 for sparks, a burning smell, a gas odor or anyone who is hurt.
  • Photograph the damage before moving anything, and write down the time you found it and who you called.

Business interruption priorities

In the first hour, work through four things. Power comes first, since wet equipment and outlets are a shock risk. Then inventory, then records, then access for staff and customers.

Lift stock, files and electronics off the floor and onto dry shelves if you can do it without stepping through standing water. Paper, cardboard and upholstered items soak up water quickly, so move them first. Your crew can advise on what to move and what to leave for extraction.

Tell the dispatcher about alarms, lockboxes, keys and building hours when you call. A crew that cannot get in cannot start.

After-hours losses in a closed building

A supply line that fails at midnight in a closed suite can run until the morning. By then the water may have reached carpet, drywall, the suite next door and the floor below. Standing water on commercial flooring does not wait for business hours.

If your alarm company or property manager reports water in the suite, call us from outside the building. Give the dispatcher the street address, the suite number and who can open the door. The EPA advises drying water-damaged areas and items within 24 to 48 hours to prevent mold growth, so a night call matters.

How the work goes in a business space

1

Assess and stop the spread

The crew confirms the source is off, checks the suite and the walls it shares, and talks through access and power.

2

Extract

Extraction wands and portable units pull water from carpet, tile and sealed concrete.

3

Map the moisture

Moisture meters and a thermal camera find wet drywall, ceiling cavities and carpet backing that look dry.

4

Dry

Air movers and dehumidifiers run until readings return to normal. Drop ceiling tiles that are soaked are removed, and some dry ones are lifted to dry the space above.

5

Clean up and repair

Debris is bagged, surfaces are cleaned, and damaged ceiling, drywall, base and flooring are repaired or replaced.

Two materials need special attention in commercial buildings. Drop ceilings hide water above the grid, where insulation and wiring sit. Commercial carpet is often glued to the slab, so water can sit between the carpet and the concrete long after the top looks dry.

The ANSI/IICRC S500 standard for professional water damage restoration is the published reference for these steps, and you can ask any company whether its crews work to S500.

Tenant and landlord responsibilities in general terms

Who pays depends on your lease, so read it. In general, a landlord is responsible for the building shell and shared systems, and a tenant is responsible for improvements, contents and the business itself, but leases vary a great deal. Ask your property manager and your insurer before you assume.

Notify the property manager the same day, in writing, even if you called the crew yourself. Keep a record of every call and photo. We do not decide who pays, and we cannot promise how any claim will be handled.

What changes the cost of commercial restoration

  • Square footage and suites reached. A single room differs from a whole suite or two suites sharing a wall.
  • Materials. Glued commercial carpet, drop ceilings, drywall, insulation and cabinetry each need different handling.
  • Water category. Clean water, gray water and sewage need different gear, disposal and disinfection.
  • Time since the loss. A night of running water is a bigger job than a morning catch.
  • Access and hours. Work around staff, customers and building hours affects scheduling.
  • Repair scope. Some jobs end after drying. Others need ceiling, drywall, paint or flooring repaired.

Documentation for your insurer

Insurance is worth a short, general note. California's Department of Insurance says homeowners policies do not cover flood damage but do cover other kinds of water damage, and coverage depends on the cause. Sudden and accidental water damage is often covered, while slow leaks and neglect often are not, and flood usually needs its own policy. Check your policy or ask your insurer, because we cannot promise how any claim will be decided.

Business losses add more to document. Photograph the water, the damaged stock and equipment, and the building. Keep receipts for emergency steps. We record moisture readings and photos as the work goes, and you can pass them to your insurer or adjuster.

Mistakes to avoid

Do not wait for the property manager to call back before shutting off water you can safely reach. Do not leave wet ceiling tiles or carpet in place to see if they dry. Do not run space heaters or plug in wet equipment to speed things up.
