Black Mold vs Mildew in Laguna Woods, CA

Agencies use the words differently, but they agree on what to do. Use this guide to decide whether a dark spot is a cleaning job or a moisture problem.

By the Laguna Woods Water Damage Pros team · Published October 29, 2026

Staring at a dark spot in a Laguna Woods, CA bathroom and wondering whether it is black mold or mildew? The label matters less than where the spot is, how big it is and why it is wet. Black mold usually means a dark or greenish-black growth, and mildew is a looser word for mold on a damp surface.

The sections below show how the agencies use the two words. A decision table then turns what you see into a next step, so you can tell a quick cleaning job from a moisture problem that needs a report or a call.

Three agencies, three uses of the word

The EPA says mildew refers to certain kinds of mold or fungus, and that it typically appears in damp places such as showers and window ledges. FEMA's mold brochure for flood recovery calls it mildew, mold in an early stage. So both treat mildew as mold, not as something safer or different.

The CDC and the California Department of Public Health take a step further. Both say the type of mold does not change what you do about it, and neither recommends testing to find out. The CDC says all molds should be treated the same with respect to potential health risks and removal.

What that means in practice

You do not need a lab to choose a next step. You need three answers: how big is it, what is it growing on and where is the water coming from? The rest of this page is built around those questions.

Why color does not settle it

The CDC describes Stachybotrys chartarum as a greenish-black mold, which is where the name black mold comes from. But many molds look dark, and many can look black, green or gray depending on age and surface. A color on its own does not tell you what the growth is.

That is also why home test kits and swabs rarely help. A result tells you the species, and the plan remains the same: dry the area and remove the growth. If you need documentation for a lender or a real estate matter, ask what the other party requires.

Cleaning the surface kind

For a small area on a hard surface, the EPA says to scrub the mold off with detergent and water and dry the area completely. The EPA says areas of about 10 square feet or less can often be handled by the household. The CDC says bleach can be used in limited amounts, while the California Department of Public Health does not recommend it, so detergent is the safer common ground.

If a spot returns after cleaning, treat that as the real finding. A recurring patch means the moisture source is still there, and cleaning again will not solve it. Consider the humidity too, since the EPA advises keeping it below 60 percent, ideally between 30 and 50 percent.

Local note

Dark spots on shower caulk or a ceiling in the Village

Laguna Woods Village buildings date from 1963 to 1985, according to the Village's own community information. Original tubs, caulk lines and exhaust fans in buildings that age can be worn, so black spots at a shower seam may signal caulk at the end of its life. If the spots are limited to caulk, replacing it is usually a maintenance job.

A bathroom ceiling stain, a soft wall or a repeating patch is a moisture issue worth reporting. Resident Services at 949-597-4600 is the number the Village lists for leaks in manors. Ask them whether the source falls on you or the mutual, because mutuals differ and we cannot decide that for you.

Why the distinction still helps a little

If the label does not change the cleanup, why bother with it? Because the words tend to signal scale. Someone who says mildew is usually describing a thin film in a wet room, while someone who says black mold is usually describing a patch that has spread or keeps coming back. Those descriptions point to different amounts of moisture.

Use the words as a rough size gauge, nothing more. A film at the shower that wipes off has probably been wet and dry in cycles. A dark patch on a ceiling or wall has probably been damp for longer, and that is the pattern the EPA ties to its 10 square foot guide for when to bring in help.

Write down what you see before you clean. Note the room, the size, the surface and the date, and take a photo. That record helps if you need to report a leak, talk to your insurer or compare the patch against what it looks like a month from now.

When it is time to call

Call a professional when the area is larger than the EPA's 10 square foot guide, when drywall is soft or wet, when sewage was involved or when you cannot find the water. The industry standard for professional mold work is ANSI/IICRC S520.
