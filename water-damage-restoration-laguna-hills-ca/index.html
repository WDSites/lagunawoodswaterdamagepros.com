Older tracts, older plumbing

The Moulton Parkway and El Toro Road neighborhoods grew up in the 1960s. Moulton Niguel Water District's history page says the first residents moved into Laguna Hills in 1965. A home that was finished that year is now more than 55 years old, so original supply lines, water heaters and drain stacks are often near the end of their service life.

The Census Bureau's 2019 to 2023 estimates put the owner-occupied rate at 71.4 percent, with a median home value of $944,000. Higher values raise the stakes on finishes, so repair scope and matching matter more than speed alone. Rebuilds are covered on our water damage repair page.

When water is spreading in Laguna Hills

Water that is still moving is the emergency. Take the steps below and call the 24/7 line, (949) 670-0939, while you do.

Stop the source

Find the shutoff at the fixture or the main valve and close it. If you cannot reach it, tell the dispatcher.

Power down wet areas

Switch off breakers to wet rooms only if you can reach the panel while standing on dry floor.

Lift and protect

Move furniture legs off wet carpet and lift valuables, papers and electronics above the water.

Keep clear of dirty water

Gray or black water needs gear. Keep children and pets out and wait for the crew.

Two water districts, two emergency contacts

El Toro Water District serves portions of Laguna Hills, and Moulton Niguel Water District also lists Laguna Hills in its service area. The district boundary by street is shown on an interactive map on the districts' websites, and we did not find a published street list.

Moulton Niguel publishes a 24/7 emergency line, (949) 831-2500, for water and sewer issues. El Toro Water District publishes a main number, (949) 837-7050, and no after-hours line that we could find. If you see water in the street or a leaking meter, call the district for your address after you call us.

The two districts also report different water hardness in their latest published reports. El Toro Water District's 2026 report lists averages of 236 and 293 mg/L for its two supplies, which is why white mineral film is common on fixtures after a leak dries.

The city's flood ordinance and big losses

Laguna Hills adopts FEMA flood maps by reference in its municipal code. The code treats restoration costing 50 percent or more of a building's pre-damage market value as substantial damage, which is handled like a substantial improvement, and it requires a site development permit for work in a Special Flood Hazard Area. We could not find a city page that names a flood zone for the city, so check the FEMA flood map for your address at msc.fema.gov.

Most leaks are nowhere near that threshold. If yours might be, ask the city building department before you start repairs. Our flood damage restoration page explains what happens when storm or sewage water enters a home.

What we handle in Laguna Hills

Callers in this part of Orange County most often need emergency water removal after a failed supply line or appliance, drying after the water is out, cleanup of wet contents and, when a leak went unnoticed, mold remediation.
