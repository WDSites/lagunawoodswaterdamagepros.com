How to Remove Black Mold in Laguna Woods, CA

For a small patch, the steps are simple and the order matters. Know where the limit is, and stop there.

Small-area stepsDry completelyKnow when to stop

By the Laguna Woods Water Damage Pros team · Published October 29, 2026

Removing black mold in Laguna Woods, CA, means taking the growth off a surface, drying the area and fixing the water that fed it. The CDC describes the species behind the name, Stachybotrys chartarum, as a greenish-black mold and says all molds should be treated the same with respect to removal. So the steps for a small patch are the same whatever color you see.

This guide is for a small area only. If you are unsure about the size, the surface or the cause, stop at step one and call.

Before you start: size it up

The EPA's rule of thumb is about 10 square feet, roughly a three-foot by three-foot patch. Areas that size or smaller can often be handled by the household. Mold over that size, mold after significant water damage and mold after sewage call for a professional.

Measure by eye with a tape if you need to, and look at the surface too. Mold on tile, metal or sealed concrete is a cleaning job. Mold on carpet, ceiling tile or soft drywall is likely a replacement job.

The removal steps for a small area

1

Protect yourself

Follow the protective equipment guidance the EPA and CDC publish for mold cleanup, and keep it on the entire job. If you cannot meet that guidance or you are unsure, stop and call a professional.

2

Isolate the area

Close the door to the room, keep other people and pets out and avoid spreading dust. Keep the work contained to the room as far as you can.

3

Scrub hard surfaces

Use detergent and water, as the EPA advises. The California Department of Public Health points to soap and water for non-porous materials.

4

Dry completely

Dry the surface and the air around it. The EPA stresses drying completely, because a damp surface invites regrowth.

5

Discard what is porous

The EPA says absorbent materials such as ceiling tiles and carpet may have to be thrown away if they become moldy.

6

Fix the cause

Mold will not grow without moisture. Find the leak or humidity source and correct it.

A word on what to leave out. Do not paint or caulk over mold, since the EPA says to clean and dry first. And do not mix cleaning products. Bleach is where the agencies differ, with the CDC saying it can be used in limited amounts and never mixed with ammonia, and the California Department of Public Health not recommending it. Detergent and water avoids the argument.

Stop and call if you notice any of these

The area is larger than about 10 square feet, or you keep finding more as you clean. The wall or ceiling is soft, swollen or wet, or the damage came from sewage. You cannot find the source of the water, or you are not comfortable doing the work with the protective gear described by the agencies.

What to do with the debris

Porous items that were moldy are thrown away rather than cleaned, as the EPA describes for ceiling tiles and carpet. Handle them as little as you can and take them out of the room without dragging them across clean areas.

After the clean-up, watch the area for a few weeks. A patch that returns means the moisture is still active, and a second round of scrubbing will not hold. At that point a moisture assessment is the next step, and water mitigation covers drying a wet structure.

Keeping it from coming back

Removing mold is half the job, and the other half is keeping the surface dry. The EPA advises keeping indoor humidity below 60 percent, ideally between 30 and 50 percent, and says the key to mold control is moisture control. A cheap hygrometer in the room tells you where you stand.

Run the bathroom fan during and after showers, fix drips under sinks promptly and dry any wet item within 24 to 48 hours, the window the EPA gives for preventing mold growth. If you see a stain spreading on a ceiling or wall with no obvious cause, assume a hidden leak until a moisture reading says otherwise.

Keep a short note of the date you cleaned, the size of the patch and a photo. If the same spot returns, you will have a record for Resident Services, your insurer or a professional.

Why a quick wipe is not enough

Mold that is wiped but not dried, or cleaned without fixing the leak, usually returns. Drying is the step people skip, since a surface can look clean while the material underneath is still damp. The EPA stresses complete drying for exactly this reason.

If a room still smells musty after you have cleaned and dried the surface, the growth may be somewhere you cannot see, such as inside a wall. That is a signal to get a moisture reading, not to scrub harder.

What a professional does differently

A professional job begins with locating moisture and defining the affected area, then containing it so spores and dust do not travel. Damaged materials are removed, surfaces are cleaned and the area is dried and checked. The industry standard is ANSI/IICRC S520, which covers procedures and post-remediation verification, and you can ask a company whether it works to that standard.

The EPA advises hiring a contractor with mold experience and checking references. The California Contractors State License Board lets you check a license online or at (800) 321-CSLB, and the City of Laguna Woods says work of $500 or more needs a licensed contractor.
