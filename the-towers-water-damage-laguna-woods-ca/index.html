Why a high-rise leak is different

In any building with units stacked above one another, water follows gravity. A failed supply line on an upper floor can soak the floor, then the ceiling below, and drain into wall cavities and shafts. Damage found in one unit may have started two floors up.

That is why a Towers job usually needs more than one door opened. We check the floors above and below, with the neighbors' permission, and we coordinate access with building management. We found no published account of water losses in The Towers, so we do not claim a typical pattern.

First-hour safety steps in a high-rise

  • Close the shut-off valve for the leaking fixture or appliance if it is safe to reach.
  • Stay away from standing water near outlets, lamps and power strips, and do not touch breakers while standing in water.
  • Do not use an elevator if water is running into the lobby or shaft. Use the stairs and tell building management.
  • Keep a path clear of wet floors and cords, because slips are a real risk on tile and stairwell landings.
  • Photograph the source and the damage with the date visible, and ask neighbors below to photograph theirs.
  • Call building management so common shut-offs can be checked.
  • For a backed-up drain, avoid contact with the water and call for professional cleanup.

Our process for a multi-floor loss

1

Map the spread

Moisture meters and a thermal camera show how far water traveled through floors, walls and ceilings.

2

Extract

Extraction wands and pumps remove standing water, and wet carpet and pad are lifted where needed.

3

Remove what cannot dry

Saturated drywall and insulation are cut out so cavities can dry.

4

Dry each affected unit

Air movers and dehumidifiers run until readings reach dry reference points, following the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard as a reference.

5

Repair

Drywall, paint and flooring are repaired after the plan is approved by you and, where it applies, building management.
