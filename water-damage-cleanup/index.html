Water damage cleanup is the removal of wet debris, damaged materials and contamination, followed by cleaning, sanitizing and deodorizing. It overlaps with drying but answers a different question. Drying asks whether the building is dry. Cleanup asks whether it is clean and safe to live in.

Safety and preparation before work begins

What to do before the crew arrives

  • Stop the source if you can reach the valve safely, and keep children and pets out of wet rooms.
  • Do not touch outlets, cords or appliances standing in water, and do not use a household vacuum on water.
  • Wear waterproof gloves and boots if you must enter, and avoid contact with dirty or smelly water.
  • Take photos of the damage and the source before anything is moved, and save damaged items for your insurer to see.

When water damage cleanup cannot wait

The water was not clean

Water from a washing machine overflow, toilet or drain carries bacteria. Contaminated materials should be removed and the area disinfected.

Belongings are wet

Books, cardboard, clothing and upholstered furniture hold water. Sorting what can be dried and what cannot is part of cleanup.

A musty smell has started

Odor is a signal that materials are still damp. Finding the damp spot matters more than masking the smell.

Visible mold spots

The EPA says mold needs moisture and that porous materials like ceiling tile and carpet may have to be thrown away if they become moldy.

What the cleanup process covers

1

2

Soaked padding, saturated drywall pieces and ruined contents are bagged and removed.

3

Surfaces are washed and treated with products suited to the water category and the material.

4

Deodorize and verify

Odors are treated at the source and surfaces are checked against moisture readings.

Cleaning follows the water category. Clean water from a failed supply line needs washing and drying. Gray water from an appliance overflow needs a disinfecting step. Black water from sewage needs protective gear and disposal of porous materials. The ANSI/IICRC S500 standard for professional water damage restoration describes those categories, and you can ask any company whether its crews work to S500.

Odor control is physical, not cosmetic. Sprays and fragrance can cover a smell, but only removal of the damp material and drying remove it. Hard water can also leave white mineral film on surfaces once they dry. El Toro Water District's 2026 report lists average hardness of 236 mg/L for the Metropolitan Water District supply and 293 mg/L for the Baker plant supply, so wiping down fixtures after a leak helps.

Contents: what can be saved

Hard, non-porous items such as glass, metal and sealed plastic can usually be washed and kept. Porous items such as cardboard, particleboard and soft furnishings soak up water and are harder to save. EPA guidance says porous materials like carpet and ceiling tile may have to be thrown away if they become moldy.

Photograph everything before it is discarded. A written list with photos helps your insurer, and it helps your mutual if the loss involves a shared wall or ceiling.

What affects the cost of water damage cleanup

  • Water category. Gray and black water need disinfection and removal of porous materials.
  • Area and rooms. More wet area means more labor and more disposal.
  • Contents. Sorting, packing and moving contents adds time.
  • Materials removed. Carpet, pad and drywall removal changes the rebuild.
  • Odor treatment. Odor that has soaked into materials takes more steps than a fresh spill.

Insurance is worth a short, general note. California's Department of Insurance says homeowners policies do not cover flood damage but do cover other kinds of water damage, and coverage depends on the cause. Sudden and accidental water damage is often covered, while slow leaks and neglect often are not, and flood usually needs its own policy. Check your policy or ask your insurer, because we cannot promise how any claim will be decided.
