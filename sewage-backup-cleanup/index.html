If sewage is coming up through a toilet, tub or floor drain in your Laguna Woods, CA home, stop using water and call. Our dispatch line, (949) 670-0939, is open 24/7, and a crew can be sent the same day when available. Until then, keep children, older family members and pets out of the room.

Sewage backup cleanup is the removal of wastewater that has flowed backward out of a drain line, along with the materials it soaked, followed by disinfecting and drying. The ANSI/IICRC S500 standard for professional water damage restoration classes this as Category 3, or black water, because it can carry harmful organisms.

How a backup starts inside the building

A backup begins when wastewater cannot move down the line and finds the lowest open path, which is usually a floor drain, a tub or the toilet nearest the blockage. Grease, wipes, tree roots and a collapsed pipe section are common culprits. In a building where several homes share a drain line, the water often shows up in the lowest unit first.

Laguna Woods Village was built in phases between 1963 and 1985, according to the Village's community information. Lines that old can crack, shift or collect buildup. We cannot tell you what pipe sits behind your wall, so ask Resident Services or your mutual about your building.

What you see and what it means

Gurgling or slow drains in several fixtures

When a toilet, tub and sink all drain slowly or bubble, the blockage is usually in a shared line, not a single fixture.

Water in the tub when you flush

The line is blocked below the tub drain. Stop flushing and stop running sinks, because every gallon you add has nowhere to go.

Water or dark residue at a floor drain

Sewage has risen as far as the lowest drain. Check nearby carpet, baseboards and cabinets for wicking.

A sewer smell that does not fade

Odor after the water is gone means contaminated material is still in the floor, wall base or pad.

Sewage backup safety steps for the first hour

  • Stop flushing and stop running sinks, showers, dishwashers and washing machines. Extra water only pushes more waste into the room.
  • Keep people and pets out of the affected rooms. Do not wade through the water, and do not run a household vacuum on it.
  • If water is near outlets, cords or the panel, stay out and cut power only if you can reach the switch without touching water. Call 911 for any sign of sparks, burning smell or a medical emergency, as the City of Laguna Woods fire page directs.
  • Call Resident Services at 949-597-4600 if you live in the Village, and call us for the cleanup. Photograph the damage before anything is moved.

Who to call for the blockage itself

Restoration crews clean up after a backup. They do not clear the line, so you also need the party responsible for the pipe. In the Village, call Resident Services first, because the blockage may be in a main line serving the building.

El Toro Water District provides wastewater service to the City of Laguna Woods, according to the district's own website. A blockage can be the building's or the district's, so ask Resident Services which it is, or call the district main line at (949) 837-7050 during office hours. We do not know of a published 24-hour district line, so do not count on one.

Away from the Village, a private plumber can scope your lateral line and clear it. Ask the plumber to tell you in writing where the blockage was, because the answer decides who pays.

How our crew handles a black water loss

1

Gear up and contain

Crew members put on gloves, boots, eye protection and respirators. The work area is separated so contamination does not travel into clean rooms.

2

Extract the water

Extraction wands and portable units pull contaminated water from hard floors and carpet. Nothing goes down a household drain.

3

Remove porous materials

Carpet, pad, baseboard and wet drywall that sewage touched are cut out, bagged and removed.

4

Clean and disinfect

Hard surfaces and framing are scrubbed with detergent, then treated with an appropriate disinfectant. HEPA vacuums pick up fine debris.

5

Dry and verify

Air movers and dehumidifiers dry the area. Moisture meters and a thermal camera confirm the structure is dry before rebuilding starts.

Why carpet, pad and drywall usually go

Porous materials absorb contaminated water and cannot be reliably cleaned once it soaks in. The CDC's flood cleanup guidance says to discard items that cannot be washed and disinfected, and its list includes carpeting, carpet padding, rugs and upholstered furniture. It also says to discard drywall and insulation that touched sewage or flood water.

Hard materials are different. Tile, sealed concrete, stone and framing lumber can usually be scrubbed and disinfected in place.

Why this is not a do-it-yourself job

Mops, towels and household cleaners spread contamination more than they remove it. The EPA's mold guidance advises getting professional help when sewage or contaminated water is involved. Wet porous materials also stay damp inside, which sets up mold if they are left behind.

Gear matters too. CDC guidance calls for rubber boots, rubber gloves and goggles during flood cleanup, and wet waste needs proper bagging and disposal. Wash hands with soap and clean water after any contact, and ask a physician about any health concern.

What changes the cost of sewage backup cleanup

  • Area and rooms reached. A single bathroom is a smaller job than a hallway, bedroom and kitchen.
  • Depth and time since the backup. Water that has sat longer soaks deeper into wall bases and subfloor.
  • Materials removed. Carpet and pad come out quickly. Drywall, cabinets and flooring add labor and rebuild work.
  • Drying days and equipment. Heavier losses need more air movers, more dehumidifiers and more monitoring visits.
  • Rebuild scope. Water damage cleanup ends when the area is clean. Replacing what was removed is separate repair work.

Insurance and who pays for a backup

Sewer backup coverage is often an optional add-on, and many policies exclude it unless you added it. California's Department of Insurance says homeowners policies do not cover flood, but cover other kinds of water damage depending on cause. Read your policy for the words sewer, drain or water backup, or ask your insurer which applies.

Responsibility also depends on where the blockage sat. Third Mutual's damage restoration policy lists fixture and mainline stoppages within the building among its causes, and the Third and United insurance disclosures describe owner responsibility for interiors and personal property. Both disclosures describe a large per-occurrence master policy deductible. Mutuals differ, and the summaries are not clear enough to state who pays in your case, so confirm with Resident Services.

Mistakes to avoid

Do not keep flushing to test the line, and do not pour chemical drain cleaner into a backed-up fixture. Do not run fans over wet sewage, because they push contaminated droplets and odor through the home. Do not keep wet carpet or pad in place to save a few days.

If the first backup clears, call anyway. A line that backed up once often will again, and water may already be inside the wall base. For storm water entering from outside, see flood damage restoration. For nearby service, our Laguna Hills and Lake Forest pages list what we cover there.
